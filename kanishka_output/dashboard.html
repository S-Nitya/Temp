📄 Research Paper · Perishable Goods MAS

Multi-Agent Negotiation
for Perishable Goods Pricing

A comparative study of three seller architectures — Static Baseline, Single-Model LLM, and a Psychological Orchestrator MAS — evaluated on Profit Capture, Velocity, and Waste Reduction in a simulated perishable goods market.

📌 Finalized Problem Statement

Perishable goods markets present a unique pricing challenge: items lose value exponentially as their expiry approaches, requiring sellers to dynamically balance profit maximization against waste minimization in real-time negotiations with heterogeneous buyers. Traditional single-agent LLM approaches to autonomous negotiation treat the entire negotiation pipeline as a monolithic task, failing to leverage specialized capabilities for buyer profiling, strategic pricing, and persuasive communication.

We propose a Tactical Orchestrator MAS framework that decomposes the negotiation pipeline into three specialized sub-agents: a RiskReader that psychologically profiles buyers from conversation history, a ListingAgentDecider that makes pricing decisions informed by the psychological profile, and a Spokesperson that crafts persuasive, context-aware messages. We evaluate our framework against (1) a Static CSV Baseline and (2) a Single-Model Monolithic LLM on metrics of Profit Capture Ratio (PCR), Information Velocity (IV), and Extraction Efficiency (EE) using a dataset of 15 perishable goods categories with 15 simulated buyer agents across 10 simulated hours.

What Each Metric Means
How to read the benchmark results at a glance.
PCR

Profit Capture Ratio

How much of the available profit margin was actually captured. PCR = 1.0 means sold at retail price. PCR > 1.0 means sold above retail (premium extraction). Higher is better.

IV

Information Velocity

Sales closed per unit of time. Measures how quickly the seller converts buyers. A high IV means fewer hours wasted in failed negotiations. Higher is better.

EE

Extraction Efficiency

Net economic value created: (Total Profit − Waste Loss) / Max Potential Profit. Captures both margin quality and waste avoidance in one number. Higher is better.

Waste Loss

Waste Loss ($)

Total cost price of inventory that was never sold and will expire. Represents pure money burned. The Hive's dynamic pricing strategy should minimize this. Lower is better.

Total Profit

Total Profit ($)

Sum of (sale_price − cost_price) across all closed deals. The raw dollar earnings above cost. Higher is better.

Items Sold

Items Sold / 15

Raw sales volume. With smart buyers (retail-anchored), selling 10+ out of 15 items under fair market conditions shows strong negotiation ability. Higher is better.
